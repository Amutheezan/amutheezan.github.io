The **single-source shortest path (SSSP)** problem is one of the important of graph theory and computer science. For decades, **Dijkstra’s algorithm** has been the go-to method. The runtime of Dijkstra’s algorithm depends on the priority queue implementation with complexity of  $$O(m + n \log n)$$,
here $$n$$ is the number of vertices and $$m$$ the number of edges.

---

The new Algorithmic Breakthrough [Refer: [Arxiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.17033)]

Recently, researchers proposed a **new algorithm** that challenges the long-standing dominance of Dijkstra. The key highlight, is reduced complexity
of the new algorithm with computational complexity of 

$$
 O\big(m \cdot \log^{2/3} n\big)
$$

This is strictly better asymptotically than Dijkstra’s $$O(m + n \log n)$$ bound, especially for **sparse graphs** where $$m$$ is closer to $$n$$.

---
We could express the time complexity as simple functions as follows:

1. $$O(n \log n + m)$$ may be actually take $$k_1 n \log n + k_2 m$$ computational units, where $$k_1,k_2$$ are problem independent constants
2. $$O(m \log^{2/3} n)$$ may be actually take $$k_3 m \log^{2/3} n$$ computational units, where $$k_1,k_2$$ are problem independent constants

In order to make the new algorithm efficient we need to identify the maximum value of $$m$$ such that the proposed approach outperforms the dijistra.
Accordingly, we could formally express the inequality to detemine the maximum value of $$m$$ as follows:

$$
k_1 n \log n + k_2, m > k_3 m \log^{2/3} n
$$

Accordingly, $$m$$ should satisfy the below constraints such that new approach to outperform the dijistra algorithm

$$
m < \frac{k_1 n \log n}{k_3 \log^{2/3} n - k_2}
$$

where $$k_1,k_2,k_3$$ are problem independent constants.

---

For simplicity let assume all the problem indenpendent constants as $$1$$, accordingly we can consider the simplified inequality,

$$
n \log n + m > m \log^{2/3} n
$$

Accordingly, $$m$$ should satisfy the below constraints such that new approach to outperform the dijistra algorithm in the simplified versions

$$
m < \frac{n \log n}{\log^{2/3} n - 1}
$$

In such cases, we could define the density of a graph with respect to the number of edges. Interestingly, 
the density (i.e., number of edges divided by number of nodes) drops below 1% at around 1,000 nodes. Refer the picture below
which show maximum possible graph density for different number of node values.

![image](https://amutheezan.com/images/GraphDensity.png)

This is generally reasonable for road networks, where not every node (i.e., intersection) is connected to every other node via road segments. 
In practice, most intersections connect to only a handful of nearby roads, making real-world road networks sparse graphs. 
This sparsity is exactly why algorithms with improved logarithmic factors, like the new SSSP method with $$O(m \log^{2/3} n)$$, 
can yield significant performance gains over Dijkstra, especially as the network scales to tens or hundreds of thousands of nodes.
